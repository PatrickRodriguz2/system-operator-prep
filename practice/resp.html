Emergency Response: practice questions

Acting when the system is already in trouble: disturbances, capacity emergencies, restoration, and losing your own tools. Domain 4 of the NERC System Operator Certification content outline. Each answer is explained and cites its source.

Question 1 · System Restoration

A Transmission Operator's area contains a nuclear plant with agreed off-site power arrangements. What must its restoration plan say about them?

  1. How the arrangements, including restoration priority, will be fulfilled
  2. How long the plant's emergency diesel generators can carry safety loads
  3. Which transmission lines the plant operator may switch without permission
  4. What reactor conditions oblige the plant to reduce its power output
Show the answer

Answer: A. The plan must describe how the Agreements or mutually agreed procedures for nuclear off-site power requirements, including priority of restoration, will be fulfilled during System restoration. Plant-side equipment and reactor limits sit outside the restoration plan.

Reference: EOP-005-3 R1.2

Question 2 · Response to System Disturbances

A Transmission Operator is operating outside an Interconnection Reliability Operating Limit (IROL) whose associated Tv is 25 minutes. What does NERC Reliability Standard TOP-001-6 require of it?

  1. The exceedance must not continue beyond 25 continuous minutes
  2. The exceedance may continue for up to 30 continuous minutes
  3. Firm load shedding must begin the moment the limit is exceeded
  4. The limit may stand until the next Real-time Assessment is run
Show the answer

Answer: A. TOP-001-6 Requirement R12 bars a Transmission Operator from operating outside an identified IROL for a continuous duration exceeding that IROL's associated Tv, which here is 25 minutes. The Glossary caps the Tv value itself at 30 minutes or less, so 30 minutes is a ceiling on how long any Tv may be, not a duration this exceedance is allowed to run.

Reference: TOP-001-6 R12; NERC Glossary - Interconnection Reliability Operating Limit Tv (IROL Tv)

Question 3 · Response to Capacity Emergencies

A Balancing Authority reports conditions that could develop into an Energy Emergency later in the day but is meeting its obligations right now. Under NERC Reliability Standard EOP-011-4, the Reliability Coordinator

  1. must declare an Energy Emergency Alert for the potential emergency
  2. may wait until the Balancing Authority is energy deficient
  3. must obtain agreement from its neighboring Reliability Coordinators first
  4. may declare only once the Balancing Authority formally requests an alert
Show the answer

Answer: A. Requirement R6 applies to a Reliability Coordinator that has a Balancing Authority experiencing a potential or actual Energy Emergency, so the duty to declare is not deferred until the shortfall arrives. A Reliability Coordinator may also initiate an alert on its own, without waiting to be asked.

Reference: EOP-011-4 R6; Attachment 1 Section A.1

Question 4 · Response to Loss of Control Center

A Balancing Authority's Operating Plan for backup functionality describes the backup site's power sources, physical and cyber security, data exchange capabilities and Interpersonal Communications. Which required element is still missing?

  1. Tools and applications giving System Operators situational awareness
  2. A maintenance schedule for the protective relays at the backup site
  3. Facility Ratings for the Elements shown on the backup displays
  4. A staffing roster naming the site's certified System Operators
Show the answer

Answer: A. R1 Part 1.2 lists five elements that the plan must summarize: situational awareness tools and applications, data exchange capabilities, Interpersonal Communications, power sources, and physical and cyber security. Only the first is absent here; relay maintenance and Facility Ratings belong to other standards, and personnel roles are handled by the transition Operating Process rather than listed as an element.

Reference: EOP-008-2 R1 Part 1.2

Question 5 · Response to Loss of Analysis Tools

A Transmission Operator will take its alarm processor out of service next Tuesday for a software upgrade. Whom must it notify about this planned outage?

  1. Its Reliability Coordinator and known impacted interconnected entities
  2. Its Reliability Coordinator, but only after the upgrade is finished
  3. Only the Generator Operators whose telemetry feeds the processor
  4. Its Regional Entity, through the next quarterly compliance report
Show the answer

Answer: A. TOP-001-6 Requirement R9 covers all planned outages of telemetering and control equipment, monitoring and assessment capabilities, and associated communication channels, and names both the Reliability Coordinator and known impacted interconnected entities. Notification after the fact defeats the purpose, which is to let those entities allow for the lost data while it is gone.

Reference: TOP-001-6 R9
