Question 1 · Protection and Control

A line Fault is cleared correctly and promptly by the faulted line's own relaying, but a transformer's protection at the same station also trips the transformer. How is the transformer operation classified?

  1. Unnecessary Trip - During Fault by the transformer protection
  2. Slow Trip - During Fault by the line protection
  3. Failure to Trip - During Fault by the transformer protection
  4. Correct backup operation, because the Fault was on another Element
Show the answer

Answer: A. The faulted line's Composite Protection System cleared the Fault without any outside help, so the transformer trip was not needed, and an unnecessary Composite Protection System operation for a Fault on another Element is an Unnecessary Trip - During Fault. Calling it backup fails because a remote or local backup operation is only correct when the faulted Element's protection fails or is slow.

Reference: NERC Glossary - Misoperation; PRC-004-6 Supplemental Material

Question 2 · Voltage and Reactive

A transformer is carrying 180 MW and 80 MVAr. What is its apparent power loading?

  1. 197 MVA
  2. 260 MVA
  3. 161 MVA
  4. 173 MVA
Show the answer

Answer: A. S = sqrt(180^2 + 80^2) = sqrt(32,400 + 6,400) = sqrt(38,800) = 197 MVA. Adding 180 and 80 arithmetically gives 260 MVA and ignores that the two legs of the power triangle sit 90 degrees apart.

Reference: Power system fundamentals

Question 3 · Electrical Fundamentals

A transmission line section has a series resistance of 6 Ω and a series reactance of 8 Ω. What is the magnitude of its series impedance?

  1. 10.0 Ω
  2. 14.0 Ω
  3. 48.0 Ω
  4. 2.0 Ω
Show the answer

Answer: A. Resistance and reactance are 90 degrees apart, so they combine as the square root of the sum of the squares: the square root of (36 + 64) is 10.0 Ω. Adding the two values arithmetically gives 14.0 Ω and overstates the impedance.

Reference: Power system fundamentals - series impedance

Question 4 · Reconfiguration and Switching

An air-break switch is fitted with an interrupter rated for magnetizing and charging current. Which operation is it suitable for?

  1. Dropping an unloaded transformer off the bus
  2. Clearing a fault on the transformer's low-side bus
  3. Opening a line carrying 90 MW of through flow
  4. Breaking the field current of a synchronous condenser
Show the answer

Answer: A. An interrupter of that class is sized for the small magnetizing current of an unloaded transformer and for line charging, so dropping an unloaded transformer is within its duty. Fault current and tens of megawatts of through flow must be broken by a circuit breaker, which carries an interrupting rating for them.

Reference: Control-room switching practice - load-break devices

Question 5 · Operating Limits

The summer Thermal Rating of a 230 kV line is set by the current at which the conductor sags to its minimum legal clearance rather than by conductor damage. How should the operator treat that value?

  1. As a valid Thermal Rating, since sag limits are part of it
  2. As a seasonal advisory that does not bind Real-time loading
  3. As a voltage limit rather than a limit on line current
  4. As a stability limit applied to the line's power flow
Show the answer

Answer: A. The Glossary defines a Thermal Rating as the maximum current a line can conduct over a specified period before permanent overheating damage or before sagging to the point of violating public safety requirements, so a clearance-driven value is a Thermal Rating. It binds Real-time loading the same as a conductor-driven value.

Reference: NERC Glossary - Thermal Rating
